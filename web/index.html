    LATENCY LAB Command-to-effect delay Not connected

    Three ways to measure how long a command takes to reach the lightstick: an automatic Bluetooth round-trip probe, a tap test for the delay you actually see, and a camera-observed flash test that excludes human reaction time.

    Bluetooth round-trip Automatic · uses TX and RX

    Sends five alternating color probes. Write is how long the device takes to acknowledge the write; Echo is how long until its response notification arrives. This measures the Bluetooth path only, not the LED's own reaction time.

      No probes yet

      Perceived effect Tap when you see it

      Watch the lightstick. The light will turn white after a short random delay — tap the button the instant you see it. The result includes your reaction time (~150–250 ms), so subtract that when judging choreography.

      Not tested yet

      Camera flash Camera-observed · no reaction time

      The light flashes white and the camera times the write until the visible change. This covers Bluetooth plus the LED, and excludes human reaction time — but it includes camera capture and frame quantization (~33–100 ms), so treat it as an upper bound. Point the camera at the lightstick and center it in the guide circle first.

      BLINK LAB Speed-to-frequency calibration Camera off

      The firmware speed value (0–255) has no documented relationship to how fast the light actually blinks. Point the camera at the lit lightstick and this lab measures its real blink rate at any speed, sweeps several speeds to fit a calibration line, and converts a target blink rate into a speed value — using a built-in formula until a sweep calibrates your own lightstick. Everything is analyzed in this browser.

      Measure a speed Camera on the lightstick

      Pick a color and speed, send the blink, then read the light's real rate. Center the lit head in the guide circle; a dim background helps. Drag the circle to move it, pinch (or Ctrl+scroll on desktop) to resize it. Speeds too fast for the camera to resolve (on-time below a few frames) show a hint instead of a measurement.

      Calibration sweep Speed → measured rate

      CAPTURE LAB Guided camera capture Camera off

      A guided capture flow that crops a fixed centered square from the live camera frame, applies a circular mask, and resizes it to 224×224 for detection. Align the Candybong head inside the circle, then capture. Everything runs locally in this browser.

      Align and capture Camera on the Candybong

      The crop square equals the circle's bounding box — exactly what you see inside the circle is what gets captured, so keep the lit head centered. Drag the circle to move it, pinch (or Ctrl+scroll on desktop) to resize it, and Reset to return to the default size and center.
